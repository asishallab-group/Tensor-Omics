# Benchmark: Rcpp vs cpp11 vs pure C (`.Call`) vs `.C` — marshalling overhead

_Generated by `bench.R` on 2026-07-31 (R 4.6.1)._

All four variants call the identical Fortran symbol `loess_smooth_2d_c` in
`build/libtensor-omics.so`. The routine is driven with large read-only
reference arrays (`x_ref`, `y_ref`) and a tiny working set
(`n_used = n_target = 100`) so compute is negligible and the only thing that
scales with size is argument marshalling.

| n_total | payload MB | rcpp ms | cpp11 ms | C ms | .C ms |
|--------:|-----------:|--------:|---------:|-----:|------:|
| 1,000,000 | 15 | 0.1 | 0.1 | 0.1 | 6.6 |
| 5,000,000 | 76 | 0.133 | 0.133 | 0.133 | 75.267 |
| 20,000,000 | 305 | 0.125 | 0.125 | 0.125 | 196.125 |

payload MB = `2 * n_total * 8 bytes` (`x_ref` + `y_ref`), the data `.C` must
duplicate on every call and the zero-copy paths do not.

## Interpretation

- **Zero-copy holds for all three `.Call` shims — Rcpp, cpp11, and pure C are at parity.** All stay flat at ~0.12 ms/call across a 20x growth in input size — that floor is R call dispatch plus the trivial O(n_target*n_used) compute, and it does *not* grow with the reference arrays. The differences between the three are below `proc.time()` resolution at these rep counts, i.e. noise, not signal.
- **`.C` pays the copy.** It scales ~linearly with payload (6.6 -> 196.1 ms as the copied data goes 15 -> 305 MB) and is ~1569x slower than the zero-copy paths at the largest size. This is the `DUP`-always behaviour that made `.Fortran`/`.C` untenable for large read-only matrices in the first place.
- **Conclusion.** Performance does not decide between Rcpp, cpp11, and pure C —
  all three are zero-copy and identical. The choice is about dependencies and
  generated-code ergonomics, not speed. Pure `.C` is the only loser: it is not
  a viable replacement for large read-only inputs.

## Outcome

**Pure C `.Call` was adopted**, and the R emitter generates it today: a `.Call` shim per
procedure in `src/generated/bindings/r/`, compiled into `build/libtensor-omics.so` by the
ordinary build, under a generated R wrapper. The decision was the one this benchmark left
open — dependencies and ergonomics — and it went to pure C because it drops the Rcpp and
cpp11 build dependencies entirely, needs no C++ toolchain, and lets the shims ride along in
the library rather than being compiled separately from R.

What this benchmark contributed was the licence to choose on those grounds: it established
that the three `.Call` variants are indistinguishable at the boundary, so no performance
argument stood in the way. And it ruled `.C` out for good — ~1569x slower at 305 MB, because
it duplicates every argument on every call.

`SERDE_CHECK.md` is the other half: performance parity is not correctness, so the two
conversions Rcpp had centralised — R int-logicals to Fortran `c_bool`, and R strings to
fixed-width column-major char buffers — were reimplemented in pure C and verified both by
round-trip and by cross-check against the Rcpp deserializer, which rules out a symmetric bug.
Those are now `tox_bool_in`/`tox_bool_out` and `tox_char_in`/`tox_char_out` in the generated
`tox_marshal.h`.
